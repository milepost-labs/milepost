The problem

Most grant tools stop at selection. Milepost starts there.

01

Did the money reach the person?

A transparent vote, then a lump sum, then silence.
02

Could they spend it on what it was for?

Tuition, seeds, shifts worked. The purpose gets lost after the transfer.
03

Can anyone prove it afterwards?

The next funder starts from zero, every time.

Milepost answers all three by tying each payment to a signed proof, and keeping every release on a public record.

How it works

Seven steps. Money moves at three of them.

  1. Contribute Money in

    Funders put money into a programme. The budget is contributions minus the protocol fee, 2.5% today and capped at 10%.

    10,000 in − 250 fee = 9,750 budget

  2. Apply

    Each applicant states how much they need. One person needs 200 for exam fees, another 5,000 for tuition. Splitting equally isn't funding.

  3. Review

    Each reviewer approves an amount, up to what was asked.

    4,2004,5004,8005,0005,000

  4. Finalize

    The award is the median of the votes once the quorum is in. Anyone can press it, and the budget can never be exceeded.

  5. Attest

    A trusted verifier, such as a school, clinic or field partner, signs that the condition was met.

  6. Release Money moves

    One valid proof unlocks one tranche. A proof can't be used twice.

  7. Spend Money out

    Where it goes depends on the payment mode: a verified payee, an escrow the recipient controls, a policy wallet, or the recipient directly.

  8. After the window

    Whatever wasn't paid out goes back to funders in proportion to what each put in. Not a "full refund": a fair share.

Guarantees and limits

What's promised, and what isn't.

Guaranteed by the contracts

  • The budget is never exceeded.Awards stop when the money does.
  • Unused money goes back to funders, in proportion.What nobody claims is swept to the treasury after a grace period.
  • One proof, one tranche.An attestation can't be reused in the same programme.
  • Standing travels with the recipient.A permanent, non-transferable record of totals received and delivered. Counts only, never a list.

Limits, stated plainly

  • First to finalize decides who's funded.Finalize is open to anyone so no applicant is stranded. When a programme is oversubscribed, the budget is protected but the ordering isn't fair.
  • Lists are advisory.The contracts keep no lists. A public index rebuilds them every few hours, and anything you act on is re-read on chain first.
  • Restricted mode is weaker than it looks.Its policy limits one signer, not the whole wallet.
  • Testnet, pre-audit.Don't put real money in yet. One attestation can unlock a tranche in two programmes that trust the same verifier, by design.
